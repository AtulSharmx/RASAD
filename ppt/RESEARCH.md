## 3. Past SIH idea PPTs: what we could find

**Honest limit:** we could **not** find a public winning or shortlisted idea PPT for a Ministry of Defence or logistics problem. Teams rarely publish them. What we did find:

- **SIH 2024 team deck, SIH1540 (AI interview system, Software).** [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/sih2024-ppt-presentation-for-smart-india-hackathon/271778038)
  - It is the same 6-slide template, filled almost only with bullets and no diagrams.
  - Its references were generic ("Elsevier, Science Direct"), and its feasibility slide was thin.
  - Lesson: do the opposite. Use visuals, concrete feasibility and real references.
- **SIH 2024 finalist team write-up.** [DEV Community](https://dev.to/macroandmicro/behind-the-scenes-key-takeaways-from-our-smart-india-hackathon-2024-experience-5glc)
  - Use infographics for the architecture, plus a detailed tech stack (prototype vs production).
  - Use "detailed flowcharts" because they "demonstrate the validity and scalability" of the solution.
- **SIH winner write-up.** [reskilll "How I won SIH"](https://blogs.reskilll.com/?p=448)
  - "A working ugly prototype beats a beautiful non-working presentation."
  - Judges valued "the solution actually addressed the problem statement" and a live demo with real data.
  - They did not care about code complexity.
- **SIH 2026 PPT guide (unofficial).** [reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/sih-2026-ppt-template-exact-format-slides-evaluators-score/)
  - Evaluators spend "2–3 minutes per submission", and "diagrams > text".
  - At most 6 bullets per slide, body text at least 14 pt.
  - Common rejections: drifting from the specific problem statement, and "just saying 'we'll use AI' without HOW".
  - Suggested weights (unofficial): problem understanding 20%, **innovation 25%**, technical feasibility 20%, impact 20%, presentation 15%.
- **SIH winner-format generator.** [GitHub varuntejreddy03/sih2025](https://github.com/varuntejreddy03/sih2025)
  - Strict 6 slides, bullets only, visual storytelling.
  - Architecture flowchart on the technical slide, plus UI screenshots or mockups.
- **College internal-hackathon templates for SIH 2026** (e.g. [this Google Slides template](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/15FMRwOKdDGnwlRGWad-VsgAg5hfBO85k/htmlpresent)): the same structure, "max 6 slides", PDF only.
- **Defence context:** DIAT Pune won SIH 2020 with "DRISHTI", an AI face/gesture recognition system ([GKToday](https://www.gktoday.in/defence-institute-of-advanced-technology-topped-smart-india-hackathon-2020/)). An SIH 2024 Ministry of Defence problem (SIH 1650) had a finalist team, GLOFzilla ([DJSCE](https://djsce.ac.in/docs/SIH%20GRAND%20FINALE%202024.pdf)). We found no public decks for either.

## 4. What good decks do (the rules we will follow)

1. **One message per slide, as a headline.** Evaluators skim for 2–3 minutes.
2. **Diagrams and screenshots over text.** Keep each pointer to 2–4 short points.
3. **Prove it is real.** A screenshot of the working prototype on the very first content slide. That is our biggest edge, because most idea decks are mock-ups.
4. **Show HOW, not "we will use AI".** Name each model and solver, what goes in and what comes out.
5. **Be concrete on feasibility.** Say what is already built, what it costs (free tools), and how it plugs into existing systems. List risks with a mitigation for each.
6. **Quantify impact.** Use numbers from the working demo, clearly tagged as a simulated scenario with placeholder costs.
7. **Use real references** (links), not "Science Direct".
8. **Stay on the exact problem statement.** The SIH26251 statement names AI/ML demand forecasting, GIS-enabled planning, IoT inventory tracking and one integrated system; each should be visible.
